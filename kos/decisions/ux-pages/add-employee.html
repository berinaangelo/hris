Add Employee — three recommendations

The HR-Admin creation form under Company → People Directory (decisions/ux-pages/people-directory.html) — the "Add" step of the v1 primary flow: "HR adds an employee once: profile + manager, an onboarding checklist, and any starting documents." What changes across the three is how much structure sits between opening the form and creating the record: one continuous form, a guided multi-step wizard, or a form paired with a live preview of the record being created.

Field scope, not yet a decision on file: this form captures only what HR actually knows at hiring time — legal name, a personal email (to send the account-setup invite), and employment facts (title, department, manager, start date, type). Birthdate, mobile, home address, and emergency contact are left for the employee to fill in on first login, per decisions/ui/my-profile-summary-plus-modal.md's existing "employee edits contact/emergency info" split. The onboarding checklist is not configured here — it's a fixed list auto-attached on creation, matching the `Employees::AttachOnboardingChecklist` step in decisions/rails-thin-controllers-organizer-interactor-pattern.md. Starting documents are optional at creation — can always be added later from Employee Detail.

Why this one

Fastest to build and fastest to use — HR already knows every fact before opening this form (it comes from the hiring decision itself), so there's nothing to progressively disclose. Seven fields plus an optional upload doesn't need pacing.

Best when

An admin adding employees regularly wants to move fast. Less forgiving for someone doing this for the first time — the whole form is visible at once, with no guidance on order.

Why this one

Breaks the form into digestible steps and makes the documents step's optional, skippable nature explicit rather than implied by a label. A Review step catches mistakes before the record is created.

Best when

Someone new to the system benefits from guidance. At seven fields total, this is arguably more ceremony than the form needs — the same "don't build more structure than the content requires" reasoning the project has used against configurable multi-step approval chains.
