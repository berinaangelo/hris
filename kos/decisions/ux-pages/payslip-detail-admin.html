Payslip Detail (admin view) — three recommendations

What an HR Admin sees when opening one employee's already-finalized payslip — reached from a finalized row in Payroll Run Detail or from an employee's own payslip history. Unlike Run Detail's editable drawer, a finalized cutoff's line items are locked (decisions/rails-db-transactions-locking-idempotency.md) — this screen is for looking up, verifying, resending, and — if a real correction is needed — voiding and reissuing, not inline editing.

Carried over, not re-decided here: same employee and cutoff shown on decisions/ux-pages/my-payslips.html (Mikaela Santos, EMP-0142, Aug 1–15, 2026) — this is the admin's view of the exact payslip the employee sees on their own My Payslips page. Figures are illustrative placeholders, not real rate-table output — real amounts come from decisions/statutory-deductions-as-editable-data-not-code.md; the loan line reflects decisions/cash-advance-vs-loan-ledger-distinction.md. Void & Reissue is shown as the correction path for all three options since there's no inline-edit surface once a run is finalized — it opens the same kind of consequential-action modal already used for Finalize on Payroll Run Detail, not a full workflow.

Void & reissue this payslip?

This marks the current payslip Voided (kept for the record, no longer downloadable by the employee) and creates a new one you can edit before it's finalized. The employee is not notified until the reissued version is finalized.

Why this one

Closest sibling to My Payslips' own breakdown, plus exactly what an admin needs on top: who can act on it and a thin audit strip answering "was this actually sent and seen" without digging elsewhere. Cheapest of the three to build — reuses the breakdown markup verbatim.

Best when

Most visits are "pull up this one payslip, confirm the numbers, maybe resend it." The audit strip is a flat, non-interactive row — fine for one fact each, but has nowhere to grow if a payslip is ever reissued more than once.

Void & reissue this payslip?

Marks v2 Voided and adds a v3 to the correction history above, editable before it's finalized. The employee isn't notified until the reissue is finalized.

Why this one

Payroll figures are exactly the kind of record that eventually gets disputed — "why is this different from what I expected" — so the correction history is promoted to a permanent on-page timeline instead of something an admin has to go dig up. Record and delivery facts sit right next to the numbers they explain, not below them.

Trade-off

Costs a fixed 280px rail on every visit even for payslips that were never touched twice — most of them, day to day — and needs its own empty state ("No corrections") for that common case, not shown here since this mockup demonstrates one that was reissued.

Marks this document Voided and opens a new one you can edit before it's finalized. The employee isn't notified until the reissue is finalized.

Why this one

Renders as the literal document the employee received — a formal itemized table, not the app's own two-column breakdown style — so a support conversation ("this is exactly what was sent to you") never has to translate between two visual languages. Admin metadata is kept deliberately outside the document sheet so the sheet itself stays print/PDF-faithful.

Trade-off

A second visual system just for this one screen — its own table style, its own spacing — that the other two options avoid entirely by reusing the app's existing breakdown component. Best suited to disputes and audits specifically, weaker as the everyday "quickly check a number" screen the other two are optimized for.
