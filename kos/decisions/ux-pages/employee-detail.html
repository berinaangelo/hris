Employee Detail — three recommendations

The HR-Admin view of one employee record, reached from decisions/ux-pages/people-directory.html — profile, org position, documents, and onboarding checklist. The admin-authored counterpart to the employee's own read-only decisions/ux-pages/my-profile.html: here HR owns every field, including employment facts that were locked on the employee's own page. What changes across the three is how editing is scoped: one combined modal, tabbed sections, or inline edit-in-place per section on a single scrolling page.

Carried over, not re-decided here: same fixed-schema rule as My Profile — no per-company/per-field configuration. Content mirrors decisions/ux-pages/my-profile.html's Mikaela Santos exactly (same Employee ID, same fields) for continuity, now with HR editing rights on employment facts too, matching the plan's "HR adds a hire once and it's correct everywhere." Status shows no badge for the default "Active" state, only for deviation (Offboarded) — per decisions/ui/badge-system-four-categories.md's own rule. Option 3 (chosen) is a hybrid: today's four sections (Personal & Contact, Org Position, Onboarding, Documents) stay on one continuous page under a "Profile" tab, each with its own inline pencil-edit — nothing hidden behind a click just to see it. "Loan Ledger" and "Benefits" sit as their own reserved top-level tabs for when Payroll v2 and the Benefits post-MVP feature land — genuinely separate modules get their own tab; today's closely-related fields don't get split apart the way Option 2 would have split them. "Offboard employee" is shown as an entry point only, no flow behind it yet.

Why this one

Revisits a tradeoff My Profile deliberately rejected (decisions/ui/my-profile-summary-plus-modal.md set aside per-section cards) — but the reasoning was specific to an employee's rare, small edits. HR edits many employees' records often, across every section, so per-section edit-in-place fits the actual usage pattern here better than it did there. Top-level tabs are reserved only for genuinely separate future modules (Loan Ledger, Benefits) — today's closely-related fields stay on one continuous page, not split apart the way Option 2 would have split them.

Best when

An admin makes frequent, varied edits across sections and wants everything visible without a click. Two independent edit states on one page is more chrome than Option 1's single modal — accepted here since it matches how often this page actually gets edited.
