Reports — three recommendations

The HR-Admin workspace under Company → Reports (decisions/ui/navigation-me-team-company.md). What changes across the three is purely how HR gets from "I want a report" to "I'm looking at it" — a persistent rail, a landing grid you drill into, or a flat row of pills. Every report itself — filters, table, export — is identical across all three; only the navigation shell around the fixed set of 7 differs.

Scope, on purpose: basic-reporting is post-MVP backlog, not committed to a version (projects/hris/features/basic-reporting/PLAN.md) — this mockup exists to compare navigation shapes ahead of build. Exactly 7 fixed views, no report builder, no dashboard configuration: Headcount Snapshot, New Hires vs. Departures, Turnover Count (raw count, not a computed rate — a formula refinement is explicitly out of scope), Leave Balances, Leave Taken Summary, Payroll Register, Statutory Contributions Summary. CSV export on every view; charts are explicitly out of scope — a table is the whole v1. Company roster of 11 across five departments (Engineering, Design, Finance, Sales, People), reused from the other Company-tab mockups. Persona: Andrea Cruz, HR Admin. All figures shown are static and illustrative.

Why this one

Groups the 7 fixed views the same way the plan itself groups them — People & Leave, Payroll — and keeps every report one click away with no landing step. Matches the reporting convention this persona already knows from Xero/Gusto-style tools (Jakob's Law), and the rail scales cleanly if the backlog ever adds an 8th view.

Best when

HR revisits the same 2–3 reports on a routine (payroll register every cutoff, headcount whenever asked) — switching stays cheap since nothing about the page re-navigates. Costs a little vertical space on the smallest report next to 7 nav rows, and on narrow screens the rail drops above the content instead of beside it.

Why this one

Each of the 7 views gets a one-line description of exactly what it answers — useful the first time HR is deciding "which of these do I even want," since names like Turnover Count vs. Leave Taken Summary aren't fully self-explanatory without a hint.

Best when

Reports is opened rarely enough that re-orienting each visit is fine, or a new HR admin is still learning the 7. Costs an extra click and a full navigation on every single visit for someone who already knows exactly which report they want — the back-to-Reports step gets old fast for a payroll-cutoff routine.

Why this one

All 7 stay visible and reachable in one row without a rail eating horizontal space — the report table itself (the payroll register's 5 columns of currency, in particular) gets the full width the comfortable-density table decision calls for.

Best when

Screen width is generous and the report names are scannable at a glance. Costs legibility as labels wrap on narrower windows — "Statutory Contributions Summary" is a long label for a pill — and unlike the rail, all 7 sit in one flat row with no People/Payroll grouping to orient by.
