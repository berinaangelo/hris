Company Reviews — three recommendations

The HR-Admin workspace under Company → Performance Reviews (decisions/ui/navigation-me-team-company.md) — the company-wide counterpart to a manager's own decisions/ux-pages/team-reviews.html. This is where HR opens review cycles and sees, across every department and every manager, where each cycle stands — not where scoring happens (that stays manager-authored, per decisions/performance-review-kpi-based-not-form-builder.md). What changes across the three is what the page organizes around: a flat filterable roster of every employee, departments rolled up with progress, or the review cycles themselves as the primary list.

Ahead of the backlog, on purpose: performance reviews/goals is still post-MVP backlog, not committed to a version — this mockup exists to compare admin-list shapes ahead of build. Viewing persona: Andrea Cruz, HR Admin (no direct reports, so nav shows Me/Company only). Company roster of 10 across four departments — Engineering (mgr. Ramon Dela Cruz): Mikaela Santos, Paolo Villanueva, Carlo Bautista, Diego Reyes; Design: Grace Lim (reports to Ramon); Finance (mgr. Ferdinand Ocampo): Isabel Torres; Sales (mgr. Miguel Santos): Bea Fernandez. Cycle content follows the fixed KPI structure on file (kpi_name / target / actual / score 1–5 / comment); "On PIP" is Caution per decisions/ui/badge-system-four-categories.md — shown here as Negative instead since, read company-wide, an active PIP is exactly the kind of deviation that badge category exists to flag (Team Reviews' own Caution choice was scoped to one manager's page, not re-decided here). All content shown is static, illustrative — this is a comparison mockup, not a wired form.

Why this one

Matches the actual data model and the actual recurring HR task — a cycle (decisions/performance-review-kpi-based-not-form-builder.md's cycle_type/ start_date/end_date) is the thing HR opens and closes. "2/10 published" answers "did H1 get done" directly, without tallying badges across a roster by hand — and PIPs read naturally as their own short-lived cycles instead of a special-cased row.

Best when

HR's job here really is running cycles to completion, not browsing people — the god-moments read ("did the company complete this half's reviews") is answered by the page's own headline numbers. Costs a click to find one specific person if you don't already know which cycle they're in, the one thing Option 1's search solves instantly.
