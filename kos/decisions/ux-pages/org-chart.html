Org Chart / Hierarchy Visualization — three recommendations

Where "who reports to whom" becomes visible without asking around — the v1 org-structure field (manager_id) surfaced as its own view, per the plan's "org structure drives directory/org-chart view" line. What changes across the three is how an HR admin (or anyone) reads the company's shape: a classic top-down tree, a scalable indented list, or a person-centered "who's above/below them" navigator.

Carried over, not re-decided here: same roster as People Directory — Ramon Dela Cruz's 5 Engineering/Design reports (Mikaela Santos, Paolo Villanueva, Carlo Bautista, Diego Reyes, Grace Lim), Ferdinand Ocampo's Finance (Isabel Torres), Miguel Santos's Sales (Bea Fernandez), and Andrea Cruz in People. The directory data shows all four department heads with no manager (—) — rather than inventing a CEO/Owner person not in the data, all three options fan them from a company-level root ("Alon Pay", the illustrative employer name already used for Job Application Form's white-labeling). Offboarded employees (Jonas Rivera) are excluded by default — an org chart's job is who reports to whom right now, not headcount history. Single-manager reporting only, no dotted-line/matrix relationships, matching the plan's scope. Search is illustrative in all three.

Why this one

The default mental model everyone already has for "org chart" — boxes and lines, shape of the company visible in one glance (Jakob's Law). Collapsing a branch is one click on the manager card itself, no separate control to learn.

Best when

Company is small enough that the tree fits on screen (or one horizontal scroll) — true for this roster today. A department with 30+ reports would need its branch collapsed by default to stay readable; not a problem yet, worth flagging for later headcount.
